Life is Hard

The model is not the question any more. The country is.

You have already done the maths on hiring in London. You know what a senior contractor costs per day and how long the last permanent hire took. You are not looking for a case for going nearshore. You made it.

What you are actually weighing is narrower and harder. Whether your data can legally sit where the team sits. Whether IP assignment holds in a foreign jurisdiction if a relationship ends badly. Whether the overlap is a real working day or four hours and a lot of goodwill. Whether the engineers are as senior as the profiles say. And whether your board will accept the answer.

Most suppliers answer this with a map and a cost-saving percentage. That is not an answer. It is the part of the conversation before the answer.

So this page does the objection work instead. Timezone, legal frame, data residency, IP, English, cost, and why Romania rather than somewhere cheaper. If any of it does not hold up, you should not sign.

The objections, answered properly

  • Timezone: a full working day, not a window. Cluj-Napoca is UTC+2 in winter and UTC+3 in summer, two hours ahead of London. Your 9am standup is our 11am and our day runs past yours. There is no overnight handover, no waiting until tomorrow for an answer, and no 6am call to catch anyone.
  • Legal frame: one jurisdiction, familiar instruments. We are SEVEN CODE DEVELOPMENT SRL, a Romanian company inside the EU single market. Contracts run under EU law with the instruments your counsel already reviews: MSA, DPA, NDA, IP assignment. Post-Brexit this is a supplier relationship, not a legal novelty, and nothing here needs a bespoke enforcement plan.
  • Data residency and GDPR: EEA by default. Romania is an EU member state, so personal data stays inside the EEA without a transfer mechanism. GDPR applies to us directly as controller or processor. Where data cannot leave your environment at all, engineers work inside your cloud accounts and we run open-weight models in your infrastructure instead of calling a hosted API.
  • IP: assigned to you, enforceable where it matters. Ownership transfers on full payment under EU law, and every engineer signs a personal IP assignment and confidentiality agreement with us. The chain of title runs unbroken from the individual to you. There is no third-country step in it.
  • English: working language, not a second language at work. Romania consistently ranks among the strongest English-speaking countries in continental Europe. Every engineer we place interviews with you in English before you commit, so you are not taking our word for it.
  • Cost: lower than a UK contractor, and not cheap. A dedicated senior engineer is typically €6,400–€8,000 per month, all in. That sits well below the loaded cost of a comparable London contractor and well above the bottom of the global market. We are not competing on price, and a supplier who is will eventually staff your project accordingly.

Nearshore, offshore or a UK contractor — an honest comparison

There is no universally right answer here. The three models fail and succeed in different places, and the correct choice depends on how much real-time collaboration your work needs.

Nearshore CEE (us)Offshore AsiaUK contractor
Timezone overlap with UKEffectively the full working day, 1–2 hours aheadTypically a few hours at the edges of each dayIdentical
Rate bandMid — materially below UK, above global entry ratesLowest of the threeHighest of the three
Legal frameEU law, GDPR direct, EEA data residencyThird-country transfer mechanism requiredUK law, simplest of the three
Communication overheadLow — same-day decisions, live pairing possibleHigher — async by necessity, written specs must carry moreLowest
Best fitLong-running product work needing daily collaborationWell-specified, self-contained workstreams at volumeShort, urgent, or highly regulated onsite work

Offshore delivery genuinely works when the scope is stable and the interface between teams is a written specification. Plenty of good engineering happens that way. It works badly when the work is discovery-heavy and the answer to a design question needs to arrive the same afternoon.

A UK contractor is the right call for short engagements, onsite requirements, or work where an extra legal hop is not worth the saving. They are the wrong call when you need three people for a year.

Our point of view

Romania is not a cost story we are apologising for. It is the reason the engineering is good.

The country has a deep technical education pipeline, a two-decade product engineering industry, and enough local and international employers that a weak engineer does not stay senior for long. Cluj in particular is a university city with a real engineering market. We compete for people against the same firms you have heard of, and we pay accordingly. That is why our rates are not the lowest available and why we do not want them to be.

The pitch we refuse to make is the one built on a savings percentage. If the only reason to work with a team is that they cost less, the relationship has no floor under it. Someone will always be cheaper next year, and you will spend the saving on rework and management attention.

Who we are not for: if your primary decision criterion is day rate, we are the wrong partner. You will find teams at a fraction of our cost and some of them are competent. Buy on rate and you should buy elsewhere. We are for teams choosing on delivery risk.

Questions we get asked before we're hired

What does nearshore development actually cost per month?

A dedicated engineer at senior level is typically €6,400–€8,000 per month, working full time on your team only. An architect usually sits between €9,600 and €12,800 per month. A dedicated pod of an architect plus two senior engineers is typically €22,000–€29,000 per month. What moves the number inside those bands is stack scarcity, the seniority level you need and whether the role requires specific domain experience. Rates are all-in: no recruitment fee, no setup fee, no separate management charge. The comparison worth running is not against a salary but against the loaded cost of a UK contractor doing the same work. More on that in nearshore versus offshore.

How much timezone overlap do we really get?

Cluj-Napoca is UTC+2 in winter and UTC+3 in summer, so two hours ahead of the UK year-round. Your 9am is our 11am and our working day extends past yours. That is a full overlap in practice: same-day code review, live pairing, decisions made in the afternoon rather than tomorrow morning. It is the single biggest operational difference between this and a delivery centre eight hours away, and it is why we do not run follow-the-sun handovers.

Why Romania rather than Poland, Ukraine or India?

Poland is a strong market and a fair comparison. It sits in the same timezone band, the same EU legal frame and generally a higher rate band, so if budget is not a factor the two are close. Ukraine has excellent engineers and a long track record, but it is outside the EU, which adds a data transfer mechanism, and the war introduces continuity questions your board will reasonably ask. India has enormous depth of talent and the lowest rates of the three, and it works well for well-specified, stable workstreams. It works less well when your work is discovery-heavy and needs answers the same afternoon. Romania sits where we think the trade-off is best for UK product teams: EU law, full day overlap, and senior engineering at a mid rate band.

Will our data leave the EEA, and how does GDPR work here?

No, not by default. Romania is an EU member state, so personal data stays inside the EEA with no transfer mechanism needed. GDPR applies to us directly and we sign a DPA as part of standard paperwork, before anyone gets access. Engineers normally work inside your own cloud accounts under your access controls, so in most engagements we are not holding your data at all. Where data cannot leave your environment even for model inference, we run open-weight models inside your infrastructure rather than calling a hosted API.

Who owns the IP, and does that hold up across borders?

You do, transferring on full payment, assigned under the contract. Two things make it hold. First, every engineer signs a personal IP assignment and confidentiality agreement with us, so the chain runs from the individual to us to you with no gap. Second, both parties are inside the EU single market, which means a familiar enforcement route rather than a third-country one. We will sign your NDA and your MSA if you have your own; we have ours if you do not.

How good is the English, honestly?

Good enough that it stops being a topic after week one. Romania consistently ranks among the strongest English-speaking countries in continental Europe, and the engineering industry here has worked in English for two decades. You do not have to take that on trust: every engineer interviews with your team in English before you commit, and you can weight communication as heavily as you like in that interview.

What are the contracting mechanics post-Brexit?

Straightforward. You contract with SEVEN CODE DEVELOPMENT SRL, a Romanian company, as a service supplier. We invoice monthly, in euros or pounds, under a standard MSA plus DPA. Services do not attract customs or import duty, and the VAT treatment for a UK business buying services from an EU supplier is the reverse-charge mechanism your finance team already applies to other EU software suppliers. No entity, no employer of record and no local registration is needed on your side.

How senior are the engineers, and can we interview them?

Senior means people who have owned services in production, not people who have contributed to one. You interview every candidate yourself, technically, run your way, and the engineer you interview is the engineer who starts. We do not swap profiles after signature. If we cannot staff your stack at a genuinely senior level, we say so on the first call instead of sending a near-enough CV. The model is the same as our staff augmentation engagements.

How fast can a nearshore team actually be running?

Two to four weeks from signed contract to engineers in your standup, covering interviews, notice periods and account provisioning. Common stacks sit at the shorter end. We aim for a first pull request inside two weeks of start, which means we plan the opening fortnight with your lead before day one. See how we work for the sequence.

We tried outsourcing before and it did not work. What is different?

The failures we get called into usually share a shape: nobody met the engineers, the contract was long enough that the supplier relaxed, and the team was managed by the supplier rather than by you. We remove all three on purpose. You interview. Terms are monthly rolling with one month's notice. The engineers report into your team lead and work your board. That does not guarantee success, but it means a bad fit shows up in week three and you can act on it.

Can we visit, or can the team come to us?

Yes to both, and we encourage it early. Cluj has direct flights from London and it is a short trip. Most clients come once at the start and find it worth the day. Engineers travel to the UK for kickoffs, quarterly planning or a hard integration week where being in the room genuinely helps. Travel is arranged at cost and is not a revenue line for us.

What if the real problem is not engineering capacity?

Then we will say so, even though it is not the sale in front of us. Sometimes a team asking for four engineers actually needs a defined scope, in which case a fixed-scope Production MVP is the better shape. Sometimes the bottleneck is an operational process rather than the product backlog, which is AI automation work. We would rather diagnose it correctly than staff the request as written. ---
