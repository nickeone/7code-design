## BLOCK: diagnosis

**H2:** The model is not the question any more. The country is.

You have already done the maths on hiring in London. You know what a senior contractor costs per day
and how long the last permanent hire took. You are not looking for a case for going nearshore. You
made it.

What you are actually weighing is narrower and harder. Whether your data can legally sit where the
team sits. Whether IP assignment holds in a foreign jurisdiction if a relationship ends badly.
Whether the overlap is a real working day or four hours and a lot of goodwill. Whether the engineers
are as senior as the profiles say. And whether your board will accept the answer.

Most suppliers answer this with a map and a cost-saving percentage. That is not an answer. It is the
part of the conversation before the answer.

So this page does the objection work instead. Timezone, legal frame, data residency, IP, English,
cost, and why Romania rather than somewhere cheaper. If any of it does not hold up, you should not
sign.

## BLOCK: package

**H2:** Nearshore, offshore or a UK contractor — an honest comparison

There is no universally right answer here. The three models fail and succeed in different places, and
the correct choice depends on how much real-time collaboration your work needs.

| | Nearshore CEE (us) | Offshore Asia | UK contractor |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Timezone overlap with UK** | Effectively the full working day, 1–2 hours ahead | Typically a few hours at the edges of each day | Identical |
| **Rate band** | Mid — materially below UK, above global entry rates | Lowest of the three | Highest of the three |
| **Legal frame** | EU law, GDPR direct, EEA data residency | Third-country transfer mechanism required | UK law, simplest of the three |
| **Communication overhead** | Low — same-day decisions, live pairing possible | Higher — async by necessity, written specs must carry more | Lowest |
| **Best fit** | Long-running product work needing daily collaboration | Well-specified, self-contained workstreams at volume | Short, urgent, or highly regulated onsite work |

Offshore delivery genuinely works when the scope is stable and the interface between teams is a
written specification. Plenty of good engineering happens that way. It works badly when the work is
discovery-heavy and the answer to a design question needs to arrive the same afternoon.

A UK contractor is the right call for short engagements, onsite requirements, or work where an extra
legal hop is not worth the saving. They are the wrong call when you need three people for a year.

**What we charge.** A dedicated senior engineer is typically **€6,400–€8,000 per month**. An architect
is typically **€9,600–€12,800 per month**. A pod of an architect plus two senior engineers typically
sits between **€22,000 and €29,000 per month**. What moves the number inside the band is stack
scarcity, seniority and whether the role needs specific domain experience. Rates are all-in and cover
employment, equipment and management overhead on our side.

will find teams at a fraction of our cost and some of them are competent. Buy on rate and you should

## BLOCK: faq

**H2:** Questions we get asked before we're hired

**1. What does nearshore development actually cost per month?** `objection: price`
A [dedicated engineer](/staff-augmentation-company-uk) at senior level is typically
**€6,400–€8,000 per month**, working full time on your team only. An architect usually sits between
**€9,600 and €12,800 per month**. A [dedicated pod](/staff-augmentation-company-uk) of an architect plus two
senior engineers is typically **€22,000–€29,000 per month**. What moves the number inside those bands
is stack scarcity, the seniority level you need and whether the role requires specific domain
experience. Rates are all-in: no recruitment fee, no setup fee, no separate management charge. The
comparison worth running is not against a salary but against the loaded cost of a UK contractor doing
the same work. More on that in [nearshore versus offshore](/nearshore-software-development-uk).

**2. How much timezone overlap do we really get?** `objection: other`
Cluj-Napoca is UTC+2 in winter and UTC+3 in summer, so two hours ahead of the UK year-round. Your 9am
is our 11am and our working day extends past yours. That is a full overlap in practice: same-day code
review, live pairing, decisions made in the afternoon rather than tomorrow morning. It is the single
biggest operational difference between this and a delivery centre eight hours away, and it is why we
do not run follow-the-sun handovers.

**3. Why Romania rather than Poland, Ukraine or India?** `objection: other`
Poland is a strong market and a fair comparison. It sits in the same timezone band, the same EU legal
frame and generally a higher rate band, so if budget is not a factor the two are close. Ukraine has
excellent engineers and a long track record, but it is outside the EU, which adds a data transfer
mechanism, and the war introduces continuity questions your board will reasonably ask. India has
enormous depth of talent and the lowest rates of the three, and it works well for well-specified,
stable workstreams. It works less well when your work is discovery-heavy and needs answers the same
afternoon. Romania sits where we think the trade-off is best for UK product teams: EU law, full
day overlap, and senior engineering at a mid rate band.

**4. Will our data leave the EEA, and how does GDPR work here?** `objection: data-residency`
No, not by default. Romania is an EU member state, so personal data stays inside the EEA with no
transfer mechanism needed. GDPR applies to us directly and we sign a DPA as part of standard
paperwork, before anyone gets access. Engineers normally work inside your own cloud accounts under
your access controls, so in most engagements we are not holding your data at all. Where data cannot
leave your environment even for model inference, we run open-weight models inside your infrastructure
rather than calling a hosted API.

**5. Who owns the IP, and does that hold up across borders?** `objection: ip-ownership`
You do, transferring on full payment, assigned under the contract. Two things make it hold. First,
every engineer signs a personal IP assignment and confidentiality agreement with us, so the chain runs
from the individual to us to you with no gap. Second, both parties are inside the EU single market,
which means a familiar enforcement route rather than a third-country one. We will sign your NDA and
your MSA if you have your own; we have ours if you do not.

**6. How good is the English, honestly?** `objection: other`
Good enough that it stops being a topic after week one. Romania consistently ranks among the strongest
English-speaking countries in continental Europe, and the engineering industry here has worked in
English for two decades. You do not have to take that on trust: every engineer interviews with your
team in English before you commit, and you can weight communication as heavily as you like in that
interview.

**7. What are the contracting mechanics post-Brexit?** `objection: other`
Straightforward. You contract with SEVEN CODE DEVELOPMENT SRL, a Romanian company, as a service
supplier. We invoice monthly, in euros or pounds, under a standard MSA plus DPA. Services do not
attract customs or import duty, and the VAT treatment for a UK business buying services from an EU
supplier is the reverse-charge mechanism your finance team already applies to other EU software
suppliers. No entity, no employer of record and no local registration is needed on your side.

**8. How senior are the engineers, and can we interview them?** `objection: seniority`
Senior means people who have owned services in production, not people who have contributed to one.
You interview every candidate yourself, technically, run your way, and the engineer you interview is
the engineer who starts. We do not swap profiles after signature. If we cannot staff your stack at a
genuinely senior level, we say so on the first call instead of sending a near-enough CV. The model is
the same as our [staff augmentation](/staff-augmentation-company-uk) engagements.

**9. How fast can a nearshore team actually be running?** `objection: timeline`
Two to four weeks from signed contract to engineers in your standup, covering interviews, notice
periods and account provisioning. Common stacks sit at the shorter end. We aim for a first pull
request inside two weeks of start, which means we plan the opening fortnight with your lead before day
one. See [how we work](/process) for the sequence.

**10. We tried outsourcing before and it did not work. What is different?** `objection: failed-before`
The failures we get called into usually share a shape: nobody met the engineers, the contract was long
enough that the supplier relaxed, and the team was managed by the supplier rather than by you. We
remove all three on purpose. You interview. Terms are monthly rolling with one month's notice. The
engineers report into your team lead and work your board. That does not guarantee success, but it
means a bad fit shows up in week three and you can act on it.

**11. Can we visit, or can the team come to us?** `objection: other`
Yes to both, and we encourage it early. Cluj has direct flights from London and it is a short trip.
Most clients come once at the start and find it worth the day. Engineers travel to the UK for kickoffs,
quarterly planning or a hard integration week where being in the room genuinely helps. Travel is
arranged at cost and is not a revenue line for us.

**12. What if the real problem is not engineering capacity?** `objection: other`
Then we will say so, even though it is not the sale in front of us. Sometimes a team asking for four
engineers actually needs a defined scope, in which case a fixed-scope
[Production MVP](/mvp-development-agency-uk) is the better shape. Sometimes the bottleneck is an
operational process rather than the product backlog, which is [AI automation](/service/ai-automation)
work. We would rather diagnose it correctly than staff the request as written.
