## BLOCK: diagnosis

**H2:** You have two quotes and no way to tell which one is honest

One agency came back with £15,000. Another came back with £120,000. You described the same product to
both of them, in the same words, on the same call. Nobody has explained the gap, and you cannot tell
whether the cheap one is missing something or the expensive one is padding.

Or you already picked. You went with the cheap team, four months went past, and what came back was a
demo that impressed nobody — not your first users, not the investor you showed it to, not you. The
code underneath is not something a second team wants to touch.

Here is the thing neither quote told you. That spread is not usually dishonesty. It is that nobody
has written down what is actually being built, so both numbers are guesses about different products.
A guess with a smaller number attached is still a guess. It just fails later.

So we do not quote a build from a conversation. We define it first, as a paid piece of work with its
own price and its own deliverable. Then the build number is a number, not a hope.

## BLOCK: howWeDeliver

**H2:** How we work, and what that protects you from

- **Scope is a separate, paid, three-to-four-week step.** It ends before you commit to a build. This is
  what stops the £15,000-versus-£120,000 problem, because by then the thing being priced is defined.
- **Senior engineers only, and the same ones throughout.** The person on your first call is the person
  who writes the code. Nothing gets handed down to a junior team once the contract is signed.
- **You approve each phase before it starts.** The build is split into phases with their own scope and
  their own number. You can stop at the end of any of them and keep everything produced so far.
- **You own it from the first commit.** The repository is yours, in your organisation, from day one.
  Not transferred at the end, not held as commercial protection.
- **A working demo every week.** Not a status deck and not a percentage. Software you can click, on a
  URL, every week. It is the only honest measure of progress and it makes bad news arrive early.
- **Your data stays in the EU.** We are an EU company delivering from Romania. GDPR posture, a DPA and
  data residency are settled during scoping, not discovered during diligence.

## BLOCK: pointOfView

**H2:** Our point of view

An MVP is not a cheap version of your product. It is the smallest thing that produces a real answer
from a real user, built well enough that a good answer can be built on rather than thrown away.

Most of the founder pain we see comes from getting that backwards. Teams either build the whole
roadmap because it was in the deck, and run out of money before anyone uses it, or they buy the
cheapest possible demo and learn nothing, because nobody trusts a product that falls over. The first
mistake costs your runway. The second costs four months and your credibility with the people you
showed it to.

We would rather spend three weeks proving the scope is wrong than five months building it. That is
also the commercially inconvenient position, which is roughly how you can tell we mean it.

**Who we are not for:** if the decision comes down to the lowest number on the quote, we will lose and
we should. We are not the cheapest team in this field and we will not pretend a fixed £15,000 buys a
product with users on it. If you want the full roadmap built before a single user touches anything,
we are also the wrong partner — we will argue with you about it in the first meeting.

## BLOCK: faq

**H2:** Questions we get asked before we're hired

**1. What does an MVP actually cost with you?** `objection: price`
Two numbers, because they are two decisions. The
[Clickable prototype + validated scope](/mvp-development-agency-uk) step is **from €8,000** over
three to four weeks. The [Production MVP](/mvp-development-agency-uk) that follows typically runs
**€40,000–€90,000** over three to five months. What moves you up that band: more than one platform,
payments or regulated data, integrations with systems we cannot test until we have access, and AI
features that need evals before they can be trusted. What moves you down: one platform, one core user
journey, and a founder willing to cut. We price the build only after scoping, so the number you get is
based on a defined product. More detail in [what an MVP costs](/hub/what-ai-automation-costs-uk).

**2. What happens if we run out of money mid-build?** `objection: price`
You stop, and you keep everything. The build runs in phases with their own scope and their own price,
and each one ends in working, deployed software rather than a half-finished branch. If funding does not
land, you end the engagement at the last completed phase, you own the code and the infrastructure, and
nothing is held back. We would rather have that conversation in month two than discover it in month
four, so tell us early — we have re-cut scope down to a smaller version one more than once, and it is a
better outcome than everybody pretending. We are also happy to invoice against phase milestones rather
than a fixed monthly figure if that matches your cash position better.

**3. Do we own the code, and could another team pick it up?** `objection: ip-ownership`
Yes to both, and the second one matters more than founders expect. The repository sits in your
organisation from the first commit, and IP transfers to you on full payment, including anything built
on top of our own internal tooling. It is in the standard contract, not an upsell. On the handover
question: we write documentation, architecture notes and a runbook for a team that has never spoken to
us, we deploy into your own cloud account, and we use mainstream frameworks rather than something only
we can maintain. If you hire an in-house team next year and they want us gone, that should take a week,
not a rewrite.

**4. How fast can you actually ship?** `objection: timeline`
The prototype and scope take three to four weeks. A production MVP is usually three to five months from
kickoff, and we have delivered inside that — Hera Health Tech went from start to a shipped MVP in three
months. Be careful with anyone promising a production app in six to eight weeks. It is achievable for a
genuinely small product, and it is a very effective way to sell a build that later needs rewriting. We
will tell you in scoping whether your idea is a six-week product or a five-month one, and it is not
always the answer the founder wanted.

**5. Who is actually writing the code?** `objection: seniority`
Senior engineers, and the same people from the scoping call to launch. We do not run an account manager
layer and we do not staff a build with juniors supervised by someone you never meet. Ask to meet the
engineer before you sign anything — we would rather you did, and a firm that will not arrange it is
telling you something.

**6. I'm non-technical. How do I know I'm not being sold more than I need?** `objection: other`
This is the right question and most founders are too polite to ask it. Three practical defences. First,
the scope document lists what we recommend cutting, in writing, before you spend anything on a build.
Second, the phases are separately priced, so you are never approving one large opaque number. Third,
ask us to explain any line item in terms of what breaks if we skip it — if the answer is vague, push
back. You can also see [how we work](/process) in detail before any of this.

**7. We hired a cheap team before and got a demo that went nowhere. Why is this different?** `objection: failed-before`
Because the failure usually happened before any code was written. A cheap build tends to be priced
against a conversation rather than a specification, so the team builds the interpretation that fits the
budget and the gap surfaces at demo time. We separate defining from building precisely so that gap
appears in week two, when it costs a fraction. We can also review what you already have and tell you
honestly whether it is salvageable or whether starting again is cheaper. Sometimes it is salvageable.

**8. Will this be investor-ready?** `objection: other`
It will be a real product with real users, which is the thing investors are actually assessing. We
instrument the MVP from day one so you can show usage, retention and funnel data rather than
screenshots. What we will not do is build features to make a demo look impressive to a room while the
product does nothing for the people using it. That is a trade founders are sometimes tempted by and it
does not survive the second meeting.

**9. What happens after launch?** `objection: after-launch`
The first weeks after real users arrive are when you learn the most, so we usually stay for a short
stabilisation period, then move to whatever fits. Some founders keep a
[dedicated senior engineer](/staff-augmentation-company-uk) on a monthly basis, typically
**€6,400–€8,000**, to keep shipping. Others take it fully in house — the handover pack is written on the
assumption that they will. Support is a retainer, it is optional, and it is not how we make the project
work commercially.

**10. Where does our data live, and can we get an NDA first?** `objection: data-residency`
Data stays inside the EEA by default. We are an EU company delivering from Romania, a DPA is part of the
standard paperwork, and we deploy into your own cloud account rather than ours. We will sign an NDA
before the first call if you would prefer, and plenty of founders do.

**11. Does our MVP need AI in it?** `objection: other`
Often not, and we will say so. AI belongs in a first version when it removes real work or makes
something possible that was not — document extraction, an assistant that saves your user twenty minutes,
an agent that handles triage. Where it earns its place we build it properly, with evals and guardrails,
through [LLM and agent development](/service/llm-agent-development). Where it is there because the deck
mentioned it, it adds cost, latency and a new way to fail in front of your first users.

**12. What if what we need turns out not to be an MVP?** `objection: other`
Then we say so on the call, before there is anything to protect. Sometimes the real problem is an
existing product that needs [AI automation](/ai-automation-agency-uk) rather than a new build, and
sometimes a founder with a technical co-founder just needs senior capacity, which is
[staff augmentation](/staff-augmentation-company-uk) rather than an agency engagement. You can see the
[product work](/case-studies) we have delivered and judge for yourself.
