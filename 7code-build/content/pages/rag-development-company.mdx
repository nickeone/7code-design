## BLOCK: diagnosis

**H2:** Five test questions answered perfectly. Then real documents arrived.

The pilot took a weekend. Load the PDFs, split on a fixed character count, embed, top-k, feed the
chunks to the model. On the five questions you tried, it was excellent. Then you pointed it at the
actual document set and it started answering confidently from the wrong contract, missing the clause
that was on the page nobody chunked cleanly, and quietly ignoring the table that held the number.

Two things are now true and neither is comfortable. Nobody can say whether a given answer is grounded
in a real passage or reconstructed from the model's own memory. And there is no retrieval eval set, so
every improvement anyone proposes is an opinion. The team is tuning a prompt to fix a search problem.

Retrieval quality is the whole game. Generation is the easy half, and it is the half that gets the
attention. We start at the other end.

## BLOCK: whatWeBuild

**H2:** What we actually build

- **Chunking and ingestion pipelines** — layout-aware parsing for PDFs, scans, spreadsheets and HTML,
  with tables and headers preserved rather than flattened. Chunk boundaries follow document structure,
  and every chunk keeps the metadata needed to filter and cite it.
- **Hybrid retrieval** — dense vectors plus keyword search, fused and tuned on your corpus. Pure vector
  search loses exact identifiers, part numbers and clause references, which is where most pilots fail.
- **Reranking** — a cross-encoder or model-based second pass over a wide candidate set, so the passages
  that reach the context window are the ones that answer the question, not merely the nearest ones.
- **Grounding and citation enforcement** — answers constrained to retrieved passages, span-level
  citations returned with the response, and a check that refuses rather than guesses when support is
  thin. An unsupported answer is a failure the system reports, not one your user discovers.
- **Retrieval eval sets** — a graded question and passage set from your own documents, scoring recall,
  precision, grounding rate and answer quality separately, running in CI so a change is measured.
- **Freshness and re-indexing** — incremental ingestion, change detection, version handling for
  superseded documents, and backfills that do not take the system offline.
- **Access control at the document level** — permissions enforced at retrieval time against your
  existing identity model, so a query never returns a passage the user cannot open directly.
- **EU-resident vector infrastructure** — the index, embeddings and document store hosted inside the
  EEA, in your own cloud account, with self-hosted embedding models where data cannot leave at all.
- **[System integrations](/service/system-integrations)** into the systems the documents already live
  in, plus [LLM and agent development](/service/llm-agent-development) and
  [AI product engineering](/service/ai-product-engineering) where retrieval is part of a wider product.

## BLOCK: howWeDeliver

**H2:** How we work, and what that protects you from

- **We measure retrieval before we tune generation.** The eval set comes first. Otherwise every prompt
  change is a guess, and teams spend months improving the half that was already working.
- **Citations are a requirement, not a feature.** If a claim cannot be traced to a passage, the system
  says so. That is what makes the output usable in a regulated or high-stakes setting.
- **Permissions are enforced in the retriever.** Filtering after generation is not access control. We
  wire document-level rules to your identity provider before the first user sees a result.
- **You meet the engineers who build it.** Named senior people from the first call to handover, with no
  account manager layer and no reassignment once the contract is signed.
- **Model and vendor agnostic.** Embedding models, vector stores and generation models are chosen per
  corpus and per data-sensitivity rule, then kept behind an interface so any of them can be replaced.
- **Your data stays in the EU.** We are an EU company delivering from Romania. Residency, retention and
  a DPA are settled before ingestion starts, and open-weight models run in your environment when needed.

## BLOCK: pointOfView

**H2:** Our point of view

Almost every failing RAG pilot we are called into has the same shape. Serious effort went into prompts
and model selection, and almost none into what gets retrieved. The pipeline splits documents on a fixed
character count, runs a single vector query, takes the top five and hopes. When the answers are wrong,
the team edits the prompt, because that is the part they can see.

The fix is rarely exotic. Parse the document properly. Add keyword search alongside vectors. Rerank a
wider candidate set. Force citations. Then build a graded eval set so the next change can be measured
rather than argued about. None of that is a research problem. It is engineering discipline applied to
the half of the system nobody enjoys.

We also think a lot of retrieval projects should be smaller. If two hundred documents answer ninety
percent of the questions, index those two hundred well instead of the whole drive badly.

**Who we are not for:** if you want a vector database installed and a chatbot pointed at everything you
own, without an eval set and without a permission model, we will decline. That build fails slowly, and
it fails in a way that damages trust in AI across your company.

## BLOCK: faq

**H2:** Questions we get asked before we're hired

**1. What does a RAG build cost?** `objection: price`
It depends on corpus size, source-format mess and permission complexity, but here are the bands. A
two-week paid retrieval review is typically **€9,000–€14,000**, the same band as the
[AI-Ready Ops Blueprint](/packages/ai-ready-ops-blueprint). A production build, a
[Production MVP](/mvp-development-agency-uk), usually sits between **€40,000–€90,000** over three to five
months. Ongoing capacity is monthly: a dedicated senior engineer **€6,400–€8,000**, a dedicated
architect **€9,600–€12,800**, a pod of an architect plus two senior engineers **€22,000–€29,000**. What
moves those most is document variety and whether access rules must be built from nothing. More detail
in [what AI work costs](/hub/what-ai-automation-costs-uk).

**2. Our pilot works on test questions and fails on real ones. Why?** `objection: failed-before`
Because the test questions were written by someone who already knew which document held the answer.
Real questions use different vocabulary, span two documents, or depend on a table your parser turned
into a wall of text. We rebuild the question set from actual user queries, measure retrieval separately
from generation, and fix whichever is losing. It is retrieval far more often than teams expect.

**3. How do you know whether an answer is grounded?** `objection: other`
We make it measurable. Answers are constrained to retrieved passages, citations are returned at span
level, and an automated check verifies each claim is supported by the cited text. Grounding rate then
becomes a metric in the eval suite alongside recall and answer quality. When support is thin, the
correct behaviour is to say so and escalate, and we build that path deliberately.

**4. What actually goes in a retrieval eval set?** `objection: other`
Real questions from your users or your support logs, each paired with the passages that genuinely
answer them, graded by someone in your team who knows the domain. A hundred to three hundred items is
usually enough to be decisive. We measure recall at k, precision, reranker lift, grounding rate and
end-to-end answer quality separately, so a regression tells you which stage broke.

**5. Do we need to fine-tune a model?** `objection: other`
Almost never for question answering over documents, and we will usually argue against it. Fine-tuning
teaches tone and format, not facts, and facts are what you are missing. It also freezes knowledge that
your corpus updates weekly. Fix chunking, hybrid retrieval and reranking first. If a genuine gap
remains after that, we will say so with the evidence.

**6. Which vector database should we use?** `objection: other`
Whichever fits your scale, filtering needs and where you are already hosted. For many corpora, Postgres
with a vector extension is enough and removes a system to operate. What matters more is metadata
filtering, permission-aware queries and re-index cost. We keep the store behind an interface so the
decision is reversible, and we write the reasoning down during the review.

**7. Our documents are confidential. Where do they go?** `objection: data-residency`
Inside the EEA by default, in your own cloud account, with a DPA as part of the standard paperwork. We
are an EU company delivering from Romania, so this is our home regime. Where documents cannot leave
your environment at all, we run self-hosted embedding and generation models inside your infrastructure.
Residency and retention are settled before any ingestion runs.

**8. Can it respect who is allowed to see what?** `objection: other`
Yes, and it must. Permissions are enforced at retrieval time against your existing identity provider,
so a restricted passage is never a candidate for a user who cannot open it. Filtering after the model
has already read a document is not access control. We model this in week one, because retrofitting it
means re-indexing everything.

**9. Who actually builds it, and how fast can you start?** `objection: seniority`
Senior engineers, named before you sign, and the same ones through delivery. Two to four weeks from
signed contract to a working team, which is our standard allocation window. The retrieval review can
usually start sooner. Ask for a working session with the engineer beforehand — we would rather you
judged us on that than on a proposal.

**10. Who owns the IP?** `objection: ip-ownership`
You do, transferring on full payment. Pipelines, prompts, eval sets, index configuration and
infrastructure code, including anything built on top of our own internal tooling. It sits in the
standard contract rather than a premium tier.

**11. What happens when the documents change, and after launch generally?** `objection: after-launch`
Ingestion runs incrementally with change detection, superseded versions are handled explicitly, and
freshness is monitored so nobody answers from a document withdrawn last month. The build ships with the
eval suite in CI, traces and a cost dashboard. A support retainer is optional, and the handover
documentation assumes your team may run it alone.

**12. Is RAG even the right approach for us?** `objection: other`
Sometimes not, and that is a cheap thing to find out early. Small stable document sets can go straight
into context. Structured questions belong in SQL against your database, not in a retrieval pipeline.
Multi-step tasks are [AI agent development](/ai-agent-development-company), and process-shaped problems
are [AI automation](/ai-automation-agency-uk). A broader build is
[AI development company UK](/ai-development-company-uk), and if you would rather add senior capacity to
your own team, that is [staff augmentation](/staff-augmentation-company-uk). You can see
[how we work](/process) before you talk to us.
