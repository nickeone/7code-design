## BLOCK: diagnosis

**H2:** Most AI projects do not fail on the technology

They fail earlier than that. Somebody picked a use case because it demoed well, not because the
process behind it was understood. The pilot ran on clean sample data. Real data arrived and the whole
thing fell over.

The second most common failure is quieter. The feature works, ships, and then slowly degrades — because
no one built an eval set, no one is watching the traces, and no one can tell you what a single run
costs. By the time a customer complains, it has been wrong for weeks.

We treat both as engineering problems, because they are. Map the process first. Test on real data
before anyone commits budget. Ship the evals, guardrails, tracing and cost dashboards with the feature,
not in a phase two that never happens.

Most clients arrive stuck between two bad options. Freelancers are fast and cheap until the work needs
more than one person or has to survive contact with production. Large outsourcing firms are reliable
and slow, and the engineers who show up are not the ones who were in the pitch.

We sit in between, deliberately.

- **Senior engineers only.** The person in your first call is the person who builds it. No account
  manager layer, no handover to juniors after signature.

  prototyped.

## BLOCK: faq

**H2:** Questions we get asked before we're hired

**1. What does an engagement typically cost?** `objection: price`
It depends on scope, but the bands are published rather than hidden. A process audit and opportunity
map, the [Operational Quick Scan](/packages/operational-quick-scan), is typically €4,500–€6,000. A
single workflow automated end to end, an [Automation Pilot](/packages/automation-pilot), typically runs
€15,000–€25,000. A production MVP is usually €40,000–€90,000, and a clickable prototype with a
validated scope starts from €8,000. A dedicated senior engineer is €6,400–€8,000 a month, an architect
€9,600–€12,800. More detail in [what AI automation costs](/hub/what-ai-automation-costs-uk).

**2. How quickly can you start?** `objection: timeline`
Two to four weeks from signed contract to a working team. We do not start recruiting after you sign,
which is why the number is that low and why it is the same number we quoted last year.

**3. Who will actually be doing the work?** `objection: seniority`
Senior engineers, and the same ones throughout. You can meet them before you commit — ask, and we would
rather you did. There is no account manager between you and the people building.

**4. Where are you based, and does that matter?** `objection: other`
Cluj-Napoca, Romania. We are an EU company delivering into the UK, Ireland, the Nordics, Benelux and
DACH. The practical effects: your working day and ours overlap almost entirely, your data stays inside
the EEA by default, and a DPA is standard paperwork rather than a negotiation.

**5. Do we own the code?** `objection: ip-ownership`
Yes, transferring on full payment, including anything built on top of our own internal tooling. It runs
in your repo and your cloud account, documented so another team can pick it up.

**6. Will we be locked into you, or into an AI vendor?** `objection: lock-in`
Neither. Everything is handed over documented and runnable. We are model agnostic — GPT, Claude,
Gemini, or an open-weight model in your own environment — and we choose per use case and per
data-sensitivity requirement, not per vendor relationship.

**7. We tried AI before and it did not work. Why would this be different?** `objection: failed-before`
Most failures we are called in to fix have the same cause: a use case chosen because it demoed well,
before anyone understood the process behind it. We map first and test on your real data before you
commit to a build. If it will not work, you find out in week two rather than month six.

**8. Do you only do AI work?** `objection: other`
No. A good share of what we do is ordinary, careful product engineering and integration work with AI in
one part of it. Sometimes the honest answer is that the AI is not the interesting bit — fixing one
integration so two systems finally agree is where the return is. We will say so.

**9. What happens after launch?** `objection: after-launch`
Every AI feature ships with a test set, guardrails, traces and a cost dashboard, so your team can see
it working and see it drift. Support retainers are available and optional. If you want to run it
yourself from day one, the handover assumes exactly that.

**10. How do we know you are any good?** `objection: other`
Ask us what went wrong on a past project — a firm that gets uncomfortable with that question is telling
you something. Then ask to speak to a client. Our Clutch reviews are public and named, and you can see
[how we work](/process) before you talk to us.
