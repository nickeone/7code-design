## BLOCK: diagnosis

**H2:** The demo worked. Production is a different question.

You have a prototype that impressed the board. On your own test set it answers well and the latency is
fine. Then it meets real documents, real edge cases and real concurrency, and the accuracy you quoted
in that meeting stops being true. Nobody on the team can tell you why it regressed last Tuesday.

Or you are one step earlier. Three suppliers have quoted the same build, the proposals are almost
interchangeable, and none of them will tell you who writes the code. The prices differ by a factor of
four and you cannot see what the difference buys.

Both problems have the same root. AI work is easy to start and expensive to operate. What separates a
demo from a system is unglamorous: an eval suite, guardrails, traces, a cost model, and someone senior
who owns the numbers. That is the part almost nobody quotes for, and it is the part we lead with.

## BLOCK: whatWeBuild

**H2:** What we actually build

- **LLM features inside an existing product** — the work of putting a model behind a real screen, with
  auth, rate limits, retries, streaming, and a fallback path when the provider has a bad hour.
- **Retrieval systems that stay grounded** — chunking, hybrid retrieval, reranking and citation
  enforcement, so an answer can be traced back to the document it came from.
- **[LLM and agent development](/service/llm-agent-development)** — tool-calling agents, approval steps
  for anything irreversible, memory and state, and a supervisor that stops a loop before it costs money.
- **Eval harnesses** — a graded test set built from your own data, wired into CI, so a prompt change or
  a model upgrade is a pull request with a pass or fail rather than a hunch.
- **Observability and cost attribution** — traces per request, spend broken down by feature and by
  customer, and alerts on latency, refusal rate and quality drift.
- **Guardrails** — input validation, output schema enforcement, prompt-injection defences, and PII
  handling decided before launch instead of after an incident.
- **The infrastructure underneath** — vector storage, queues, caching, deployment pipelines and
  [system integrations](/service/system-integrations) with the systems you already run.
- **[AI product engineering](/service/ai-product-engineering) end to end** — where there is no product
  yet, we do the design, the build and the operations as one team.

## BLOCK: pointOfView

**H2:** Our point of view

Model choice is the least interesting decision in an AI project. It changes every few months, and if
your system is built properly, changing it is a config edit and a test run. The decisions that actually
determine whether the thing works are boring: what you retrieve, how you evaluate, where you put a
human, and what happens when the model is confident and wrong.

This is why the quotes you are comparing look so different. A price that only covers the happy path
will always beat a price that includes evals, guardrails, tracing and a cost model. The difference
arrives later, usually as an incident, and it is paid by your team rather than the supplier.

We would rather lose a deal on price than win it by quoting for a prototype and calling it a system.

**Who we are not for:** if you want the cheapest possible build and you already know exactly what to
build, we are the wrong partner. We will spend the first two weeks questioning the plan, and we do not
staff projects with junior engineers to reach a number.

## BLOCK: faq

**H2:** Questions we get asked before we're hired

**1. What does AI development actually cost with you?** `objection: price`
It depends on scope, data quality and how much of your stack we have to touch, but here are the bands.
A two-week paid architecture review is typically **€9,000–€14,000**, and it is priced the same as the
[AI-Ready Ops Blueprint](/packages/ai-ready-ops-blueprint). A full [Production MVP](/mvp-development-agency-uk)
usually sits between **€40,000–€90,000** over three to five months. Ongoing capacity is per month: a
dedicated senior engineer is **€6,400–€8,000**, a dedicated architect **€9,600–€12,800**, and a pod of
an architect plus two senior engineers **€22,000–€29,000**. What moves those numbers is integration
count, regulatory scope and how clean your data is. More detail in
[what AI work costs](/hub/what-ai-automation-costs-uk).

**2. We have three quotes and they look identical. How do we compare them?** `objection: other`
Ask each supplier four questions in writing. Who are the named engineers, and will they still be on the
project in month four? What is in scope for evaluation — is there a graded test set, and who builds it?
What is the estimated running cost per thousand requests? And what happens to accuracy when the model
version changes? The answers separate quotes faster than any price comparison. Most of the gap between
a cheap number and ours is those four items.

**3. Our prototype works in the demo and fails in production. Can you fix it?** `objection: failed-before`
Usually, yes, and the diagnosis is rarely the model. The common causes are retrieval that was never
tuned on real documents, no evaluation set so nobody can measure a change, and prompts carrying logic
that belongs in code. We start with a two-week review that reproduces the failures on your own data.
You get the diagnosis in writing, and it is useful even if your team does the remediation.

**4. Who actually writes the code?** `objection: seniority`
Senior engineers, named before you sign, and the same ones through delivery. The person in your
technical call is the person on the build. We do not run a bench of juniors behind a senior front, and
we will put you in a technical session with the engineer before there is a contract if you ask.

**5. How fast can you start?** `objection: timeline`
Two to four weeks from signed contract to a working team, which is our standard allocation window. The
architecture review can usually start sooner because it needs fewer people. First production release
depends on scope, but a focused AI feature is typically live for real users within three months.

**6. You are in Romania. Does that work for a UK company?** `objection: other`
It works well and has done for years. We are an EU company delivering from Cluj-Napoca, on Central
European Time, which overlaps the UK working day almost entirely. Projects run in English, contracts
are EU law, and GDPR is our home regime rather than an export requirement. Most UK clients meet us on
video and never notice the border.

**7. Where does our data go?** `objection: data-residency`
It stays inside the EEA by default, and a DPA is part of the standard paperwork rather than an
add-on. Where data cannot leave your environment at all, we run open-weight models inside your own
infrastructure instead of calling a hosted API. We settle residency, retention and the training-opt-out
position during the architecture review, before any code is written.

**8. Who owns the IP?** `objection: ip-ownership`
You do, transferring on full payment. That covers the code, the prompts, the eval sets and the
infrastructure configuration, including anything we build on top of our own internal tooling. It is in
the standard contract, not a premium tier.

**9. Will we be locked into you, or into a model vendor?** `objection: lock-in`
Neither, by design. Everything runs in your cloud account and is documented for handover. We keep model
calls behind an internal interface so a provider swap is a configuration change validated by the eval
suite. Some clients keep us for the next feature; others take it in house after the first release,
which is a fine outcome and one we plan for.

**10. What happens after launch?** `objection: after-launch`
Every AI feature ships with an eval set in CI, guardrails, traces and a cost dashboard, so your team
can see quality and spend without asking us. We offer a support retainer, but it is optional and the
handover documentation assumes you may not want it. If quality drifts, the alert reaches you first.

**11. Will this sideline our in-house engineers?** `objection: sideline-our-team`
The opposite is the goal. We work inside your repositories, your review process and your standards, and
we pair with your engineers on the parts they will own. If you would rather add senior capacity to your
own team than outsource a project, that is
[staff augmentation](/staff-augmentation-company-uk) and we do that too.

**12. Do you only build AI, or the product around it?** `objection: other`
Both, and the second is usually the larger part. Most of an AI product is ordinary software done well.
We also build [AI agent development](/ai-agent-development-company) and
[RAG development](/rag-development-company) engagements, and
[AI automation](/ai-automation-agency-uk) work where the problem is operational rather than
product-shaped. You can see [how we work](/process) before you talk to us.
