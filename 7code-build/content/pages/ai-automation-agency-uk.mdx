**H2:** You already know which processes are broken

Someone on your team rebuilds the same report every Monday morning. A headcount was signed off last
year because a process could not scale, not because the work needed a person. A tool was bought,
rolled out, and half the team still works around it in a spreadsheet.

None of that is a technology problem. It is a process nobody has mapped end to end. And that is why
most automation projects fail — they move the mess somewhere more expensive, then break the first time
real data arrives.

We start by mapping. Then we automate one thing properly, prove it works on your real data, and only
then scale it.

## BLOCK: whatWeBuild

**H2:** What we actually build

- **Workflow automation** — the multi-step processes that currently live in someone's head and three
  browser tabs. Intake, triage, routing, approval, reporting.
- **AI agents that do real work** — not a chatbot on your website. Agents that read a document, decide
  something, act in a system, and hand the edge cases to a person.
- **Document and data extraction** — invoices, contracts, forms, emails. Structured output your
  existing systems can consume, with a confidence score attached.
- **[System integrations](/service/system-integrations)** — the connective work that makes any of the
  above possible. APIs, middleware, data sync between tools that were never designed to talk.
- **Internal tools and dashboards** — so the people who own the process can see what the automation is
  doing without asking engineering.
- **Evals, guardrails and cost controls** — shipped with the automation, not added later. This is the
  difference between a demo and something you can run a business on.

## BLOCK: pointOfView

**H2:** Our point of view

The interesting question is almost never "can this be automated". Nearly everything can. The question
is whether the process is worth keeping in the first place.

A lot of manual work exists because of a decision somebody made five years ago under constraints that
no longer apply. Automating that work preserves the decision and makes it permanent. Sometimes the
right answer is to delete the process, not automate it — and an agency paid to build things is
structurally unlikely to tell you that.

We would rather run a three-week scan that ends in "don't build this" than a six-month project that
ends in something nobody uses.

**Who we are not for:** if you want a team that ships exactly what it is told without asking whether
the thing should exist, we are the wrong partner. We will push back, in the first meeting, before
there is a contract to protect.

## BLOCK: faq

**H2:** Questions we get asked before we're hired

**1. How much does AI automation actually cost?** `objection: price`
It depends on the number of systems involved and how clean your data is, but here are the bands we
work in. A process audit and opportunity map — the [Operational Quick Scan](/packages/operational-quick-scan) —
is typically **€4,500–€6,000** over two to three weeks. Automating a single workflow end to end, an
[Automation Pilot](/packages/automation-pilot), typically runs **€15,000–€25,000** over six to ten
weeks. Digitising a whole department, the [Department Automation Sprint](/packages/department-automation-sprint),
is usually **€40,000–€65,000** over ten to sixteen weeks. We break the cost down by workflow during the
scan, so you can drop anything that does not pay for itself. More detail in
[what AI automation costs](/hub/what-ai-automation-costs-uk).

**2. We tried an AI tool last year and it did not work. Why would this be different?** `objection: failed-before`
You are not unusual. Most of the failures we are called in to fix have the same cause: someone picked a
use case because it demoed well, not because the process behind it was understood. The pilot worked on
clean sample data and fell over on the real thing. We map the process first and test on your actual
data before you commit to a build. If it will not work, you find out in week two.

**3. How long before we see anything?** `objection: timeline`
The scan produces a deliverable in two to three weeks. A first automated workflow is usually live in
six to ten weeks from kickoff. We deliberately pick an early win your team can see working, rather than
disappearing for six months and returning with a platform.

**4. Who will actually be doing the work?** `objection: seniority`
Senior engineers, and the same ones throughout. The person who runs your scan is the person who builds
the automation. We do not have an account manager layer and we do not hand the build down to juniors
once the contract is signed. If you want to meet the engineer before you commit, ask — we would rather
you did.

**5. Will we be locked into you?** `objection: lock-in`
No. You own the code, the prompts, the eval suites and the infrastructure config, and it runs in your
cloud account. Everything is documented so another team can pick it up. We are model agnostic, so you
are not tied to one AI vendor either. Some clients keep us on for new workflows; plenty take it in
house after the first pilot, which is a fine outcome.

**6. Will you replace our operations team?** `objection: sideline-our-team`
That is not what this is. The pattern we see is a team spending most of its week on work that does not
need judgement, and the rest on work that does. Automation takes the first part. In practice the people
who run the process become the people who own the automation — they are the only ones who know what a
wrong output looks like.

**7. Who owns the IP?** `objection: ip-ownership`
You do, transferring on full payment. That includes anything we build on top of our own internal
tooling. It is in our standard contract, not an upgrade.

**8. Our data is sensitive. Where does it go?** `objection: data-residency`
We are an EU company delivering from Romania, so your data stays inside the EEA by default and a DPA is
part of the standard paperwork. Where the data cannot leave your environment at all, we run open-weight
models inside your own infrastructure instead of calling a hosted API. We settle this in the scan, not
after the build.

**9. What happens after it goes live?** `objection: after-launch`
Every automation ships with a test set, guardrails, traces and a cost dashboard, so your team can see
it working and see it drift. We offer a support retainer, but it is optional and it is not how we make
the project viable. If you want to run it yourself from day one, the handover documentation assumes
exactly that.

**10. We do not have clean data. Is this premature?** `objection: other`
Sometimes, and we will say so. Poor data quality is the most common reason to slow down, and the scan
is where it surfaces. Often the honest answer is a smaller first step — fixing one integration so two
systems finally agree — before any AI is involved. That work is less exciting and it is frequently
where the return is.

**11. Do you work with companies outside the UK?** `objection: other`
Yes. We work with companies across the UK, Ireland, the Nordics, Benelux and DACH. Delivery is from
Cluj-Napoca, which overlaps the UK working day almost entirely, and we run projects in English. Most
engagements start with a video call, so location has not been a practical issue for years.

**12. What if we need more than automation?** `objection: other`
Then we say so. We also do product engineering and [staff augmentation](/staff-augmentation-company-uk),
and quite often what starts as an automation conversation turns out to be a product problem or a
capacity problem. We would rather tell you that than sell you the thing we were asked about. You can
see [how we work](/process) and the [operations work](/expertise/operations) we have done.
