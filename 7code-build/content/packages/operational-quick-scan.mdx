## BLOCK: whatYouGet

**H2:** What lands on your desk

Five named artefacts. All written, all yours, delivered whether the conclusion flatters us or not.

- **Process map** — every operational workflow in scope, documented as it actually runs. Not the
  version in the handbook. The version with the spreadsheet and the WhatsApp group in it.
- **Cost-of-work register** — each process scored on hours per month, salary cost attached, error rate
  where measurable, and the rework those errors generate.
- **Automation opportunity map** — every process ranked by what automating it is worth. Each entry
  carries a build estimate, an expected return, a confidence level, and the systems it would touch.
- **Blockers list** — the data quality, [system integrations](/service/system-integrations) and access
  problems to solve before any [AI automation](/service/ai-automation) works. Named, sized, owned.
- **Recommended order** — what to automate first, second and third, and why. Sequencing matters more
  than selection, because the first build sets the pattern the rest inherit.

We present it live to you and your process owners, then hand over a document you can circulate
internally without us in the room.

## BLOCK: howItRuns

**H2:** Two to three weeks, and what it costs your team

**Week 1 — observation.** We interview the people who run the work, not only those who manage it. Six
to eight sessions of 45 minutes, one per process owner. Your COO or operations lead spends roughly
three hours with us across the week.

**Week 2 — measurement.** We pull the numbers behind what we were told: ticket volumes, system logs, a
few months of records. Mostly our work. You need one person to grant read access, about two hours.

**Week 3 — ranking and readout.** We build the opportunity map, price each candidate, and present it.
The readout runs 90 minutes with whoever decides. The written deliverable follows 48 hours later.

Total time from your side is usually under 12 hours. That is the honest number, and it is why the scan
works for companies that cannot spare a project team.

## BLOCK: price

**H2:** What it costs and what happens next

**Typically €4,500–€6,000.**

What moves it inside that band: the number of processes in scope, how many systems hold the data, and
whether the work spans one department or several. A single-department scan with four or five processes
sits at the bottom. A cross-functional scan touching finance, operations and customer service sits at
the top. We fix the price before we start.

**If you want to proceed after the readout**, the usual next step is an
[Automation Pilot](/packages/automation-pilot) on the highest-value workflow from the map. We credit
the scan fee against the pilot if you commit within 60 days. Taking the map to another firm or building
it in house is a legitimate outcome, and the document is written to support it.

## BLOCK: faq

**H2:** Questions before you book

**1. Why pay for an audit when other firms do it free?** `objection: price`
Because a free audit is a sales document. It ends in a proposal, and it has to, or the firm giving it
away loses money. Ours ends in a ranked list that sometimes says "automate nothing this year". We can
write that sentence because you paid for the work. At **€4,500–€6,000** it is the cheapest way to avoid
a six-figure mistake. More detail in [what AI automation costs](/hub/what-ai-automation-costs-uk).

**2. What if the answer is that nothing is worth automating?** `objection: other`
Then we say so, in writing, with the numbers that led us there. It happens. Usually it means the volume
is not there yet, or the process is about to change anyway. You still get the process map and the
cost-of-work register, which most operations teams have never had in one place.

**3. How much of my team's time does this take?** `objection: timeline`
Usually under 12 hours in total across the whole engagement. One 45-minute interview per process owner,
about three hours from your operations lead, two hours of system access, and a 90-minute readout.

**4. Who actually does the work?** `objection: seniority`
Senior engineers, and the same people who would build the automation afterwards. That is deliberate. An
estimate is only useful from the person who has to honour it. See [how we work](/process) and the
[operations](/expertise/operations) engagements behind it.

**5. Do we have to use you for the build?** `objection: lock-in`
No. The deliverable is written so another firm or your own team can act on it. It names systems, volumes
and integration points rather than 7code products.

**6. Our data is a mess. Is it too early for this?** `objection: other`
No — that is what the blockers list is for. Poor data quality is the most common reason to slow down,
and the scan is where it surfaces cheaply. Often the honest first move is fixing one integration so two
systems finally agree, before any AI is involved.

**7. Where does our data go during the scan?** `objection: data-residency`
We are an EU company delivering from Romania, so anything we handle stays inside the EEA. A DPA and an
NDA are signed before the first interview. Most scans need read access and exports, not production copies.

**8. What if the scope is bigger than one department?** `objection: other`
We flag it in week one rather than at the end. If the honest answer is a full department rebuild, the
[Department Automation Sprint](/packages/department-automation-sprint) is the right shape and we say so.
Wider picture on our [AI automation agency](/ai-automation-agency-uk) page.
