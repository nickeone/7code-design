## BLOCK: whatYouGet

**H2:** What lands on your desk

Five named artefacts. The last two are the ones that decide whether this survives after we leave.

- **Connected workflows** — the department's core processes automated end to end and joined up, so
  output from one becomes input to the next without a person retyping it. Built as
  [AI automation](/service/ai-automation) or
  [LLM and agent development](/service/llm-agent-development) where judgement is genuinely needed, and
  as plain deterministic code where it is not.
- **Integrations with the systems you already run** — your CRM, ERP, finance system, ticketing, document
  store. These [system integrations](/service/system-integrations) are usually the largest part of the
  work and the part other vendors underestimate.
- **Internal tooling and dashboards for the process owners** — the screens the department actually
  works in. Queues, exception handling, approvals, and a live view of what the automation did and why,
  without anyone asking engineering.
- **Training for the team** — sessions run with the people who do the work, not a video library. They
  learn the new operating model, the exception paths, and how to tell when something is wrong.
- **A documented operating model** — who owns which process, what the escalation path is, what the SLAs
  are now that volume is not people-bound, and what changes in each role. This is the artefact that
  turns a delivery into a way of working.

Every workflow ships with the production layer as standard: eval sets, guardrails, tracing, and a cost
dashboard. Code and infrastructure live in your own repo and cloud account throughout.

## BLOCK: howItRuns

**H2:** Ten to sixteen weeks, and what it costs your team

**Weeks 1–3 — map and sequence.** We document every process as it runs today, agree the target operating
model, and sequence the build. One 45-minute interview per role, plus three two-hour workshops with the
department head. Your department head spends around ten hours in this phase.

**Weeks 4–10 — build in slices.** We deliver workflow by workflow, each one live before the next starts.
Fortnightly hour-long demos with the department head and process owners. Between demos we need about
three hours a week from two named process owners to judge outputs and resolve edge cases.

**Weeks 10–14 — integrate and shadow.** Workflows are connected, internal tooling goes live, and the
department runs the new path alongside the old one. Busiest phase for your team: two process owners at
about a day a week each, reviewing the comparison.

**Weeks 14–16 — training, cutover and operating model.** Training runs with the whole department, usually
two half-days. We cut over, stay close for two weeks, and hand over the operating model with the runbooks.

Total time from your side is usually 120 to 150 hours across four months, concentrated in the department
head and two process owners. We name those people in week one. A sprint without them named is a sprint
that slips.

## BLOCK: price

**H2:** What it costs and what happens next

**Typically €40,000–€65,000.**

What moves it inside that band: the number of workflows, how many systems need integrating and whether
they have usable APIs, the volume of historical data to migrate, and whether the department operates in
one country or several. Four workflows across modern SaaS tools sits at the bottom. Eight workflows
spanning a legacy ERP, multiple entities and a compliance regime sit at the top.

We price after a scoping phase, not before. If the department is not yet mapped, an
[Operational Quick Scan](/packages/operational-quick-scan) is the cheapest way to a firm number, and we
credit the fee against the sprint if you proceed within 60 days.

**If you want to proceed**, we agree the workflow list and the sequence in writing first. The contract
prices each slice, so you can stop after any slice with something working rather than something half
built.

## BLOCK: notForYouIf

**H2:** Don't buy this if

- **You have not automated anything yet.** Start with an
  [Automation Pilot](/packages/automation-pilot) on one workflow. It proves the approach on your data,
  it teaches both sides how the other works, and it costs a quarter as much to find out we are wrong
  about each other.
- **The department is about to be reorganised.** A documented operating model built around roles that
  will not exist in six months is a wasted deliverable. Sequence the reorganisation first, then automate
  the shape you land on.
- **You need it live in six weeks.** Ten to sixteen weeks is the honest range for a department, and
  compressing it means cutting the shadow-running and training phases. Those are the two phases that
  decide whether the department actually adopts it.

## BLOCK: faq

**H2:** Questions before you book

**1. What does a sprint cost and what changes the number?** `objection: price`
**€40,000–€65,000** over ten to sixteen weeks. It moves on the number of workflows, how many systems need
integrating and whether they expose usable APIs, the data to migrate, and the number of entities
involved. We price after scoping, and the contract prices each slice separately. Wider context in
[what AI automation costs](/hub/what-ai-automation-costs-uk).

**2. How does this compare to hiring?** `objection: other`
A sprint is a one-off cost against a permanent one. Five hires is a recurring annual commitment plus
recruitment, onboarding and the management layer above them. It is not always the better answer. Where
the work needs judgement, hire. Where the work is volume, automate.

**3. Can the department keep running while you build?** `objection: timeline`
Yes, and that is why we build in slices and shadow-run before cutover. Each workflow goes live on its
own and the old path stays available until the new one has proven itself.

**4. How much time does this take from our people?** `objection: timeline`
Usually 120 to 150 hours across four months. The department head carries around ten hours in mapping,
then an hour a fortnight. Two named process owners carry the rest, peaking at about a day a week each
during shadow running.

**5. What if our systems have no API?** `objection: other`
Common, and it is priced in. We have built against legacy systems before, including at scale. Options
are database-level integration, file-based exchange, or a controlled interface layer. We assess which is
safe during scoping and tell you if one system makes the whole sprint uneconomic.

**6. Who owns the result?** `objection: ip-ownership`
You do, on full payment. Code, prompts, eval sets, infrastructure configuration and documentation, in
your own repo and cloud account throughout the build. That includes anything built on top of our
internal tooling. It is standard contract, not an upgrade.

**7. What happens after cutover?** `objection: after-launch`
The operating model document, runbooks and dashboards are written so your team runs it without us. We
stay close for two weeks after cutover as standard. A support retainer is available and optional — it is
not how we make the project viable. Some clients instead take one of our engineers on
[staff augmentation](/staff-augmentation-company-uk) to keep building internally.

**8. Should we do a readiness assessment first?** `objection: other`
Sometimes. If the blocker is fragmented data or an unclear target architecture, the
[AI-Ready Ops Blueprint](/packages/ai-ready-ops-blueprint) answers that before you commit this much
budget. If the processes are understood and the systems are known, go straight to scoping. See
[how we work](/process), our [operations](/expertise/operations) engagements, and the wider
[AI automation agency](/ai-automation-agency-uk) practice.
