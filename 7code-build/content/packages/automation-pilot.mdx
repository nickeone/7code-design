## BLOCK: whatYouGet

**H2:** What lands on your desk

Seven named artefacts. The automation is one of them. The other six are why it is still running in a
year.

- **The working automation** — one or two workflows, live in production, processing real volume. Built
  as [AI automation](/service/ai-automation) or
  [LLM and agent development](/service/llm-agent-development) depending on what the work actually needs,
  with [system integrations](/service/system-integrations) into the tools you already run.
- **The eval suite** — a test set built from your own historical cases, including the awkward ones.
  Every change is measured against it before release. This is how you know a model update did not
  quietly make things worse.
- **Guardrails** — input validation, output constraints, confidence thresholds, and an explicit
  escalation path. Edge cases go to a named human queue, not into a void.
- **Tracing** — every run recorded end to end. What came in, what the model decided, which tools it
  called, what went out. Searchable by your team, not only by ours.
- **Cost dashboard** — spend per run and per month, broken down by step, with alerts. You will know
  what the automation costs before finance asks.
- **Handover documentation** — runbook, architecture notes, prompt and configuration inventory, and the
  failure modes we found with what to do about each one. Written for an engineer who has never met us.
- **The code, in your repo and your cloud account** — from the first week, not at the end. You own the
  code, the prompts, the eval sets and the infrastructure configuration.

## BLOCK: howItRuns

**H2:** Six to ten weeks, and what it costs your team

**Weeks 1–2 — grounding.** We confirm the process end to end with the people who run it, agree success
criteria in numbers, and get access to systems and historical data. Your process owner spends around six
hours here. Whoever controls system access spends two or three.

**Weeks 3–6 — build on real data.** We build against your actual records, not samples, and demo weekly.
Each demo is 45 minutes with the process owner. Between demos we need about two hours a week from them
to judge outputs and settle edge cases. That judgement is the part we cannot do for you.

**Weeks 6–8 — shadow running.** The automation runs alongside the people doing the work. Outputs are
compared, disagreements logged, thresholds tuned. Your team works as normal and reviews a daily
comparison, about 20 minutes a day for one person.

**Weeks 8–10 — cutover and handover.** We move it into production with guardrails live, run handover
with your team, and walk your engineers through the repo. Two sessions of 90 minutes.

Total time from your side is usually 40 to 50 hours, most of it the process owner. Shorter pilots
compress the shadow phase, never the eval work.

## BLOCK: price

**H2:** What it costs and what happens next

**Typically €15,000–€25,000.**

What moves it inside that band: how many systems the workflow touches, whether one workflow or two are
in scope, how clean the historical data is, and whether decisions need a compliance review step. One
workflow across two well-documented systems sits at the bottom. Two workflows across legacy systems with
no API sit at the top. The fee is fixed once scope is agreed, and the scope document lists what is out
as clearly as what is in.

**If you are not certain which workflow to pick**, run an
[Operational Quick Scan](/packages/operational-quick-scan) first. It costs less and prevents the
expensive version of this mistake.

**If it works**, the usual next step is widening to the rest of the department, which is the
[Department Automation Sprint](/packages/department-automation-sprint). Plenty of clients stop after the
pilot and run it themselves. The handover pack assumes exactly that.

## BLOCK: notForYouIf

**H2:** Don't buy this if

- **You want a demo for a board meeting.** A production pilot spends most of its budget on the parts
  nobody sees in a demo: evals, guardrails, tracing, shadow running. If the goal is to show something
  impressive next month, this is an expensive way to get it.
- **The process changes every quarter.** Automating a process that is still being redesigned means
  paying twice. Stabilise it first, or automate the part that is settled and leave the rest manual.
- **Nobody will own it afterwards.** The pilot needs a named process owner who will judge outputs during
  the build and keep judging them after we leave. Without that person, it degrades quietly. If the
  underlying issue is architecture and data readiness, the
  [AI-Ready Ops Blueprint](/packages/ai-ready-ops-blueprint) comes first.

## BLOCK: faq

**H2:** Questions before you book

**1. What does a pilot cost and what changes the number?** `objection: price`
**€15,000–€25,000** over six to ten weeks. It moves on the number of systems involved, whether it is one
workflow or two, the state of the historical data, and whether a compliance review step is required. We
fix the fee once scope is agreed. Full breakdown in
[what AI automation costs](/hub/what-ai-automation-costs-uk).

**2. Our last pilot never made it to production. What is different?** `objection: failed-before`
Most stalled pilots share a shape: clean sample data, no eval set, no way to tell when the output was
wrong, no cost visibility. We build on your real records from week three and ship the eval suite,
guardrails, tracing and cost dashboard as part of the work. Shadow running proves it against your own
team before cutover.

**3. When do we see something working?** `objection: timeline`
A working demo on your data by week three or four, then weekly. Shadow running starts around week six.
Production cutover is week eight to ten depending on scope.

**4. Who builds it?** `objection: seniority`
Senior engineers, the same ones from the first call through to handover. No account manager layer, no
handover to juniors after signature. If you want to meet the engineer before committing, ask. See
[how we work](/process) and our [operations](/expertise/operations) engagements.

**5. Where does the code live?** `objection: lock-in`
Your repo and your cloud account, from the first week. You own the code, the prompts, the eval sets and
the infrastructure configuration. We are model agnostic, so you are not tied to one AI vendor either.

**6. What happens when the model gets it wrong?** `objection: other`
It gets caught or escalated. Guardrails validate inputs and constrain outputs, confidence thresholds
route uncertain cases to a human queue, and every run is traced. The eval suite catches degradation
before your customers do.

**7. Will this remove jobs on the team?** `objection: sideline-our-team`
The pattern we see is a team spending most of the week on work needing no judgement, and the rest on
work that does. The pilot takes the first part. In practice the person who ran the process becomes the
person who owns the automation, because they are the only one who recognises a wrong output.

**8. Where does our data go?** `objection: data-residency`
We are an EU company delivering from Romania, so data stays inside the EEA by default and a DPA is
standard. Where records cannot leave your environment at all, we run open-weight models inside your own
infrastructure rather than calling a hosted API. This is settled before the build, not after. More on
our approach on the [AI automation agency](/ai-automation-agency-uk) page.
