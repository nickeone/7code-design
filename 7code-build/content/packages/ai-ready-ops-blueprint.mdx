## BLOCK: whatYouGet

**H2:** What lands on your desk

Five named artefacts, written to be read by a CTO and approved by a board. Both audiences get a version
they can actually use.

- **Target architecture** — a documented end state for the systems that would carry AI workloads.
  Data flows, integration points, where inference runs, where records live, and what has to change from
  today's estate to get there.
- **Data readiness assessment** — a per-source verdict on the data you would need. Completeness,
  structure, freshness, ownership, and whether the source can be trusted for automated decisions.
  Written as a red-amber-green with the reasoning attached, not just the colour.
- **Prioritised use-case roadmap** — every candidate scored on business value, build cost, delivery
  risk and data dependency. Sequenced across four quarters, with the prerequisites each item inherits
  from the one before it.
- **Model and vendor recommendation with a lock-in assessment** — which models suit which workload, at
  what cost per unit of work, and what it would take to move off each one. Hosted APIs and open-weight
  options running in your own environment are both priced.
- **Governance and data-residency position** — a document your board can sign. Where data goes, who
  approves a model change, how outputs are reviewed, what gets logged, and how GDPR obligations are met.

Underneath these sits an inventory of the current estate, the
[system integrations](/service/system-integrations) that would need building, and a cost model for
running [AI automation](/service/ai-automation) and
[LLM and agent development](/service/llm-agent-development) workloads at your volume.

## BLOCK: howItRuns

**H2:** Four to six weeks, and what it costs your team

**Weeks 1–2 — estate and data.** We review the current architecture with your CTO or IT lead, then work
through the data sources one at a time. Two workshops of two hours with the technical owner, plus short
sessions with whoever holds each system. What we need most is read access to schemas and sample exports.

**Weeks 3–4 — use cases and economics.** Three or four 90-minute sessions with operations and commercial
leads to surface and challenge candidate use cases. In parallel we build the cost model and test model
options against your real data shapes.

**Weeks 5–6 — architecture, governance and readout.** We write the target architecture and the
governance position, then present the blueprint. The readout runs two hours with the decision group. A
board-ready summary follows, plus the full technical document.

Time from your side is usually 20 to 25 hours across six weeks, concentrated in your CTO or IT lead.
Operations leads spend around four hours each. We name the sessions that matter before we start.

## BLOCK: price

**H2:** What it costs and what happens next

**Typically €9,000–€14,000.**

What moves it inside that band: the number of data sources assessed, how many systems make up the
current estate, and whether governance has to satisfy a regulator as well as a board. A single-entity
company with five or six sources sits at the bottom. A group with several operating companies, mixed
cloud accounts and a compliance obligation sits at the top. Price is fixed before we start.

**If you want to proceed after the readout**, the roadmap names its own first item and prices it. Usually
that is an [Automation Pilot](/packages/automation-pilot) on the highest-value use case, or a short
integration project to fix a data source first. We credit the blueprint fee against the first build if
you commit within 90 days.

## BLOCK: faq

**H2:** Questions before you book

**1. What does this cost, and what makes it move?** `objection: price`
**€9,000–€14,000** over four to six weeks. The band moves on the number of data sources, the number of
systems in the current estate, and whether governance has to satisfy a regulator. We fix the fee once
scope is agreed. Wider context in [what AI automation costs](/hub/what-ai-automation-costs-uk).

**2. How is this different from a consultancy's AI strategy engagement?** `objection: other`
We build the systems we recommend, so every number in the roadmap is an estimate we would have to
honour. That constrains what we can write. The architecture is drawn by people who have shipped
production AI, including RAG pipelines with human review steps.

**3. Our proof of concept failed. Will this tell us why?** `objection: failed-before`
That is one of the first things we look at. The cause is usually one of three: a use case chosen because
it demoed well, a data source that could not be trusted for automated decisions, or an architecture with
no eval or observability layer. The fix differs in each case.

**4. Who from our side needs to be involved?** `objection: timeline`
Your CTO or IT lead carries most of it, at roughly 20 to 25 hours across six weeks. Operations and
commercial leads spend about four hours each. Whoever owns each data source gives us one short session.

**5. Will you recommend a vendor you have a relationship with?** `objection: lock-in`
No. We are model agnostic and we do not resell. The recommendation compares hosted APIs against
open-weight models in your own environment, prices both at your volume, and states the exit cost of
each. Lock-in is assessed explicitly, because it is usually the part nobody writes down.

**6. Can this satisfy our board and our data protection obligations?** `objection: data-residency`
That is what the governance and data-residency position is for. We are an EU company delivering from
Romania, so EEA residency is the default and a DPA is standard. Where data cannot leave your
environment, the architecture uses open-weight models running inside your infrastructure.

**7. Who owns the blueprint afterwards?** `objection: ip-ownership`
You do, on full payment. It is written so another firm can execute against it. It names systems,
volumes, integration points and decision criteria rather than 7code products.

**8. What if the roadmap turns out to be bigger than we can fund?** `objection: other`
Then it gets sequenced honestly rather than compressed. The roadmap is built so the first quarter
stands alone and returns something, whether or not the later quarters are funded. If the realistic shape
is a full department rebuild, the
[Department Automation Sprint](/packages/department-automation-sprint) is where that lands. You can also
see [how we work](/process) and our wider
[AI automation agency](/ai-automation-agency-uk) practice.
